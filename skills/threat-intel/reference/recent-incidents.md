## Recent blockchain hack anatomy (2024–2026)

**Reading key.** "Impact" is the reported value extracted or put at risk at the time of the incident, **not necessarily the permanent net loss**. "Outside precursor" means something a tester could inspect before the loss from public code, chain state, repositories, or shipped software. It does not mean anyone had already identified the flaw. "Scoped catch" states a test hypothesis, not a claim that passive recon would have prevented the incident.

### Incident casebook

|---|---|---|---|
| **2024-03-26: Munchables** | On-chain game; malicious insider and privileged contract change | A developer associated with the project manipulated deposit-accounting storage through an upgradeable contract path, then withdrew against the fabricated balance. About **$62.5m** was exposed; control of the funds was subsequently returned. The reported developer links do **not** establish the circulating North Korea attribution ([Rekt](https://rekt.news/munchables-rekt); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/third-party-blast-bridges-disabled-after-munchables-loses-62-million-in-exploit/)). | **Yes, architectural:** the proxy, March 21 upgrade, and unverified implementation were inspectable on-chain, although the insider's intent was not. **Scoped catch:** alert on implementation changes, require verified code, and test whether an authorized upgrader can create an unbacked deposit balance ([Rekt](https://rekt.news/munchables-rekt)). |
| **2024-05-14: Sonne Finance** | Lending protocol; empty-market accounting and precision loss | The attacker took advantage of a scheduled VELO-market launch, permissionless execution after its timelock, and a known Compound-v2-fork donation/rounding weakness. Approximately **$20m** was extracted; the roughly $6.5m Sonne said it "saved" was not a recovery of stolen funds ([Sonne postmortem](https://medium.com/@SonneFinance/post-mortem-sonne-finance-exploit-12f3daa82b06); [CertiK analysis](https://www.certik.com/blog/sonne-finance-incident-analysis)). | **Yes:** the market proposal, queued change, fork lineage, and new-market code were public. **Scoped catch:** fork the pending deployment and test an adversarial first depositor and donation at the smallest share denominations before enabling collateral ([Sonne postmortem](https://medium.com/@SonneFinance/post-mortem-sonne-finance-exploit-12f3daa82b06); [CertiK analysis](https://www.certik.com/blog/sonne-finance-incident-analysis)). |
| **2024-05-31: DMM Bitcoin / Ginco** | Exchange and wallet-management provider; developer-tooling social engineering | An actor posing as a recruiter supplied a Ginco employee with a malicious Python "employment test" linked from GitHub. After compromising the employee and obtaining session information, the actors impersonated them in Ginco communications and **likely** manipulated a legitimate DMM transaction request; **4,502.9 BTC, valued by investigators at $308m**, was stolen. The FBI describes that final manipulation as likely, not directly proven in every detail ([FBI, DC3 and Japan NPA](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-dc3-and-npa-identification-of-north-korean-cyber-actors-tracked-as-tradertraitor-responsible-for-theft-of-308-million-from-bitcoindmmcom); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/297822/over-300-million-in-bitcoin-stolen-from-japanese-exchange-dmm-bitcoin)). | **Not reliably from outside:** a GitHub-linked lure and the employee's personal copy existed, but the record does not establish that an independent tester could have found and connected them before theft. **Scoped catch:** test whether developer-session compromise or altered provider-to-exchange instructions can change a withdrawal without an independent, authenticated transaction confirmation ([FBI, DC3 and Japan NPA](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-dc3-and-npa-identification-of-north-korean-cyber-actors-tracked-as-tradertraitor-responsible-for-theft-of-308-million-from-bitcoindmmcom)). |
| **2024-07-18: An Indian exchange (via third-party custody provider Liminal)** | Exchange and custody integration; signer-view/payload integrity failure | The exchange reported a mismatch between the transaction shown in the custody provider's interface and what signers actually authorized; the transaction transferred control of a multisig wallet and **more than $230m** was lost. How the payload was replaced, and responsibility for the mismatch, remained contested; it is unsound to label this a proven custody-platform compromise (preliminary incident report and contemporaneous press coverage of the custody dispute). | **Limited:** the multisig/vendor architecture could frame a test, but no pre-theft, externally visible payload mismatch is established. **Scoped catch:** in a test wallet, compare independently decoded calldata, destination, operation, and implementation changes at every signer and provider boundary; reject any display/signature divergence (per the preliminary incident report). |
| **2024-08-06: Ronin bridge** | Bridge; upgrade initialization and zero-quorum logic | A proxy upgrade skipped `initializeV3`, leaving total operator weight at zero and therefore making the withdrawal approval threshold zero. Roughly **$12m** was withdrawn in a white-hat episode; the ETH was reported returned and the USDC return was expected, with a bounty arranged ([Three Sigma analysis](https://threesigma.xyz/blog/exploit/ronin-network-12m-exploit-analysis)). | **Yes, but a short window:** the upgrade and resulting storage state were on-chain about 48 minutes before withdrawal. **Scoped catch:** run an automatic post-upgrade storage/invariant check that asserts nonzero operator weight and rejects signature-free bridge withdrawals ([Three Sigma analysis](https://threesigma.xyz/blog/exploit/ronin-network-12m-exploit-analysis)). |
| **2024-09-03: Penpie** | Yield protocol; malicious-market callback and reentrancy | The attacker registered a fake Pendle market containing a malicious SY contract. A reward-harvesting function without reentrancy protection could then be driven back into deposit logic, with flash loans amplifying extraction to **$27.35m** ([Penpie postmortem](https://blog.penpiexyz.io/penpie-post-mortem-report-1ac9863b663a)). | **Yes, architectural:** permissionless registration had been introduced in May, while the older harvesting contract sat outside that feature's audit scope. **Scoped catch:** register a deliberately adversarial market in a forked test environment and fuzz callbacks across harvesting, deposits, and withdrawals ([Penpie postmortem](https://blog.penpiexyz.io/penpie-post-mortem-report-1ac9863b663a)). |
| **2024-10-16: Radiant Capital** | Lending protocol and multisig operators; employee endpoint compromise | A ZIP presented through a message impersonating a former contractor contained a decoy Penpie-analysis PDF and the **INLETDRIFT** macOS backdoor. Multiple developer devices were compromised; malicious signing occurred while interfaces showed benign information. Radiant put the theft at approximately **$50m** ([Radiant incident update](https://medium.com/@RadiantCapital/radiant-capital-incident-update-e56d8c23829e)). | **No confirmed outside warning:** the spoofed contractor domain and staged contracts are retrospective indicators, not a documented pre-incident alert. **Scoped catch:** with participating signers, test downloaded-document handling, endpoint-to-wallet isolation, and an independent display of the exact transaction being signed ([Radiant incident update](https://medium.com/@RadiantCapital/radiant-capital-incident-update-e56d8c23829e)). |
| **2025-02-21: Bybit / Safe** | Exchange cold wallet and third-party signing interface; targeted frontend supply-chain compromise | Attackers compromised a Safe developer machine and served malicious JavaScript targeted to Bybit's Safe wallet and signers. It changed the data signed while preserving a benign-looking proposal, causing a `delegatecall` that replaced the wallet implementation and enabled theft of approximately **$1.46bn**. The precise initial compromise of the developer machine remains unknown ([NCC Group technical analysis](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/); [Bybit timeline](https://www.bybit.com/en/learn/this-week-in-bybit/bybit-security-incident-timeline)). | **Only in the narrow malicious-publication window:** the modified web asset was retrievable, but conditional targeting made ordinary monitoring less reliable. **Scoped catch:** compare served bundles with approved build hashes from multiple sessions, and independently decode and approve `operation`, target, and transaction hash outside the web UI ([NCC Group technical analysis](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/)). |
| **2025-05-11 demand; May 14 disclosure: Coinbase** | Exchange support operation; paid insider access and subsequent customer-fraud exposure | Coinbase said an actor **appeared to pay multiple support contractors or employees** for access to customer and internal information. The affected support personnel could not access customer funds, passwords, or private keys. **$180m–$400m was Coinbase's preliminary estimate of remediation and voluntary reimbursements, not an amount of crypto stolen from its wallets** ([Coinbase SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978825000094/coin-20250514.htm)). | **No established remote precursor:** improper access was detected internally, not through a documented public signal. **Scoped catch:** using seeded accounts and a cooperating support team, test least-privilege data views, bulk-access alerts, and whether support-assisted identity workflows permit account takeover ([Coinbase SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1679788/000167978825000094/coin-20250514.htm)). |
| **2025-05-22: Cetus** | Sui DEX; third-party math-library overflow check | A faulty left-shift overflow check in code used for liquidity calculations let the attacker create economically false liquidity and withdraw real assets. About **$223m** was exploited; approximately **$162m was frozen**, which must not automatically be described as returned or recovered ([Cetus postmortem coverage](https://crypto.news/cetus-dex-releases-post-mortem-outlines-recovery-plan-following-223m-exploit/)). | **Yes, code-level:** the dependency and deployed Move behavior were available for review, though the flaw was not an already published warning. **Scoped catch:** run boundary-value and conservation-property tests against the actual compiled library and pool deposit/withdrawal paths, including extreme shift inputs ([Cetus postmortem coverage](https://crypto.news/cetus-dex-releases-post-mortem-outlines-recovery-plan-following-223m-exploit/)). |
| **2025-07-09: GMX V1** | Perpetuals and liquidity pool; cross-contract reentrancy and stale accounting | An attacker-controlled contract passed through a legitimate order-keeper callback, re-entering before short-position accounting settled and distorting GLP pricing. Approximately **$42m** was exposed or moved. GMX subsequently described the funds as secured and paid a **$5m bounty**; the record does not justify treating $42m as permanent theft ([Rekt and GMX's reported analysis](https://rekt.news/gmx-rekt)). | **Yes, retrospectively auditable:** the callback path and a 2022 change that introduced the weakness were public, although the exact variant had not been flagged. **Scoped catch:** diff the old fix, then fuzz attacker-controlled callback recipients against cross-contract accounting invariants ([Rekt and GMX's reported analysis](https://rekt.news/gmx-rekt)). |
| **2025-09-24: SBI Crypto** | Mining-pool operator; **unresolved wallet/signing access** | Approximately **$24m** moved without authorization from wallets associated with operations across five chains. Public reporting did **not** establish whether this was key theft, production infrastructure compromise, insider action, or another route; a confident "hot-wallet key compromise" label would be conjecture ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/sbi-crypto-rekt)). | **No documented pre-theft precursor:** the cross-chain outflows were observable once underway, but were not established as an advance warning. **Scoped catch:** in a cooperative assessment, map key holders and withdrawal authorities, then test multi-chain approval separation and alert latency; passive recon cannot validate the undisclosed entry mechanism ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/sbi-crypto-rekt)). |
| **2025-11-03: Balancer V2** | Liquidity protocol; rounding-direction and invariant failure | Carefully selected tiny balances made scaling round down materially relative to pool value; repeated swaps compounded the error and distorted BPT valuation. Check Point estimated **$128.64m** drained across six networks ([Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/)). | **Yes, code-level:** the pool math and forked deployments were inspectable, not a known public exploit signal. **Scoped catch:** property-test value conservation and invariant monotonicity at minimum balances, mixed decimals, and long atomic swap sequences across forks ([Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/)). |
| **2025-12-24–26: Trust Wallet extension** | Shipped wallet application; dependency-to-release supply chain | An attacker used an exposed Chrome Web Store publishing credential to release malicious extension **v2.68** outside the normal approval path. The build accessed wallet secrets and enabled unauthorized transactions affecting roughly **2,520 wallets and $8.5m**. Trust Wallet assessed a link to the earlier Sha1-Hulud npm campaign with **high confidence**, but described parts of the initial exposure sequence as still under investigation ([Trust Wallet incident update](https://trustwallet.com/blog/company/trust-wallet-browser-extension-v268-incident-community-update)). | **Yes after the malicious release, not necessarily before compromise:** the extension package and attacker-controlled referenced domain could be inspected; the stolen credential was not shown to be publicly exposed. **Scoped catch:** continuously diff the store-delivered artifact against approved, reproducible CI builds and alert on new wallet-secret access or external domains ([Trust Wallet incident update](https://trustwallet.com/blog/company/trust-wallet-browser-extension-v268-incident-community-update)). |
| **2026-01-08: TrueBit** | Legacy token/bonding-curve contract; arithmetic overflow | An unprotected addition in Solidity 0.5.3 purchase-price math wrapped for an extreme input, enabling near-free TRU minting and redemption for ETH. The principal drain was about **$26.2m**; a smaller copycat extraction was reported separately ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/truebit-rekt)). | **Yes, with extra effort:** the old, unverified on-chain bytecode and compiler era were observable, but the source was not verified on Etherscan. **Scoped catch:** decompile or obtain an authorized build, then property-test extreme purchase amounts and the round-trip buy/burn payout ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/truebit-rekt)). |
| **2026-01-31: Step Finance** | Treasury and executive key holders; endpoint compromise | Step said executive devices were compromised; attackers changed Solana stake authority and withdrew approximately **261,932 SOL**, valued at **$27.3m** in Rekt's on-chain account. Step cited roughly **$40m** in broader losses, while the difference was not reconciled there; about **$4.7m** in other positions was reportedly recovered. The precise device-infection or approval route is unpublished ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/step-finance-rekt)). | **No confirmed advance public signal:** authority transfers became visible as the theft unfolded. **Scoped catch:** a cooperative assessment can test executive-device isolation, independent stake-authority approvals, and alerts on authority changes; a remote scanner cannot determine the undisclosed initial access ([Rekt investigation](https://rekt.news/step-finance-rekt)). |
| **2026-03-22: Resolv** | Stablecoin issuer and signing infrastructure; contractor-credential-to-cloud chain | A credential retained by a contractor from a compromised third-party project gave attackers repository access. A malicious workflow exposed cloud credentials, ultimately granting signing authority; attackers minted **80m unbacked USR** and extracted approximately **$25m in ETH**. Resolv later neutralized about **46m USR**, not the same thing as recovering $25m in ETH ([Resolv postmortem](https://resolv.xyz/blog/resolv-postmortem-march-22-2026-incident)). | **No demonstrated public credential leak:** the dangerous trust path was principally internal. **Scoped catch:** with repository/cloud scope, verify contractor offboarding, workflow permissions, key-policy changes, and independent on-chain mint limits; public inspection can at least identify concentration of mint authority ([Resolv postmortem](https://resolv.xyz/blog/resolv-postmortem-march-22-2026-incident)). |
| **2026-04-01: Drift** | Solana derivatives protocol; social-engineered governance signing | A months-long operation culminated in misleading, pre-signed **durable-nonce** approvals, takeover of Security Council authority, listing of fabricated collateral, and oracle-backed borrowing. Approximately **$285m** was drained. Investigators also reported compromised contributor devices; attribution and the exact contribution of each access route should not be reduced to a single "smart-contract bug" ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist); [Nexus Mutual incident report](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/drift-protocol-incident-report)). | **Partly:** governance configuration and some preparation were on-chain, but deception of signers was private. **Scoped catch:** inspect zero-timelock authority changes and durable-nonce persistence; in a supervised signing drill, require independently decoded instructions and explicit expiry/revocation of unused approvals ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist)). |
| **2026-09-06: Liquid Network** | Bitcoin sidechain and bridge-like peg infrastructure; consensus/runtime verification-cache bug | An Elements rangeproof-cache key concatenated variable-length fields without unambiguous boundaries. A crafted proof could be mistaken for a cached valid one, enabling creation of unbacked L-BTC and withdrawal of roughly **4,000 BTC, valued near $320m**. Blockstream reported **3,400 BTC returned**, with roughly **602 BTC outstanding** in its September assessment; it did **not** identify stolen federation keys as the cause ([Blockstream technical assessment](https://blog.blockstream.com/liquid-network-security-incident-assessment/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/bitcoin-based-liquid-network-says-320-million-withdrawn-hack-2026-09-07/)). | **Yes, code-level:** the relevant public Elements changes, including an earlier fix, were available before the incident. **Scoped catch:** diff cache-verification changes and test whether distinct typed tuples can collide in serialized cache keys on a private test network ([Blockstream technical assessment](https://blog.blockstream.com/liquid-network-security-incident-assessment/)). |
| **2026-09-17: Nostra** | Starknet money market; oracle-source and collateral-pricing failure | A thin NSTR market was manipulated into an extreme price. With the oracle's source threshold configured at one, the distorted observation influenced the accepted price and enabled roughly **$3.5m** in borrowing against overvalued NSTR. Final recovery was not established in the cited September 24 analysis ([BlockSec analysis](https://blocksec.com/blog/web3-security-multicall-router-nostra-exploits)). | **Yes:** source count, collateral rules, and the thin market's liquidity and transactions were observable, though the precise attack sequence became clear retrospectively. **Scoped catch:** stress-test collateral prices against single-source manipulation, shallow liquidity, and cross-source divergence before accepting a borrow limit ([BlockSec analysis](https://blocksec.com/blog/web3-security-multicall-router-nostra-exploits)). |

The selection includes an exchange-support data theft because it tests a real crypto-company entry vector, but **Coinbase's $180m–$400m estimate is not counted as stolen crypto** below. It also includes a mining-pool operator without pretending its unpublished initial access is known. No on-chain analytics-firm incident with comparably supported 2024–September 2026 entry-mechanism evidence is inserted merely to fill a victim-type slot.

Because recovery reporting is incomplete and valuations differ, a defensible, comparable "net USD permanently lost" ranking cannot be calculated from these cases.

### Remotely observable precursors: ranked within this case set

These counts are **non-exclusive opportunities to investigate before loss**, not counts of publicly reported warnings or proof that a passive scanner would have identified the exploit. A case can contribute to several rows. Ties are ordered by testing actionability; "observable" includes public bytecode that requires reverse engineering.

| Rank | Precursor to test | Cases in this 20-case set | Agent interpretation |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Publicly inspectable implementation or configuration contained the failing assumption** | **10**: the seven on-chain-logic cases, plus [Munchables' proxy](https://rekt.news/munchables-rekt), [Liquid's Elements code](https://blog.blockstream.com/liquid-network-security-incident-assessment/), and [Nostra's oracle configuration](https://blocksec.com/blog/web3-security-multicall-router-nostra-exploits) | Prioritize code, bytecode, deployed storage, and protocol parameters over "scan all hosts." Public availability is not evidence the flaw was obvious. |
| 2 | **A governance action, market launch, or privileged upgrade changed the risk boundary** | **4**: [Munchables](https://rekt.news/munchables-rekt), [Sonne](https://medium.com/@SonneFinance/post-mortem-sonne-finance-exploit-12f3daa82b06), [Ronin](https://threesigma.xyz/blog/exploit/ronin-network-12m-exploit-analysis), [Drift](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist) | Treat each state transition as a new assessment window, even if the underlying code was previously audited. |
| 3 | **Arithmetic or value-conservation behavior near extreme inputs** | **4**: [Sonne](https://www.certik.com/blog/sonne-finance-incident-analysis), [Cetus](https://crypto.news/cetus-dex-releases-post-mortem-outlines-recovery-plan-following-223m-exploit/), [Balancer](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/), [TrueBit](https://rekt.news/truebit-rekt) | Queue boundary-value property tests for rounding, shifts, overflow, first deposits, and repeated operations. |
| 4 | **Attacker preparation appeared on-chain before extraction** | **3**: contracts created before July 18 by the Indian-exchange attacker, [Drift durable-nonce staging](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/north-korean-hackers-attack-drift-protocol-in-285-million-heist), and [Nostra pool preparation](https://blocksec.com/blog/web3-security-multicall-router-nostra-exploits) | Useful as a corroborating trigger, but often low-specificity until connected to a target or authority. |
| 5 | **A publicly exposed market-creation or callback boundary accepted adversarial behavior** | **3**: [Sonne's permissionless queued execution/new market](https://medium.com/@SonneFinance/post-mortem-sonne-finance-exploit-12f3daa82b06), [Penpie's market registration](https://blog.penpiexyz.io/penpie-post-mortem-report-1ac9863b663a), [GMX's attacker-controlled callback recipient](https://rekt.news/gmx-rekt) | Model the external participant as hostile, then test every reachable callback and state transition. |
| 6 | **An old fix or fresh patch offered a variant-analysis target** | **2**: [GMX's 2022 bug-fix lineage](https://rekt.news/gmx-rekt) and [Liquid's public pre-incident Elements change](https://blog.blockstream.com/liquid-network-security-incident-assessment/) | Diff the change, articulate its invariant, and search neighboring functions for the same violated assumption. |

| 8 | **Unverified implementation or legacy bytecode limited normal review** | **2**: [Munchables](https://rekt.news/munchables-rekt) and [TrueBit](https://rekt.news/truebit-rekt) | Do not equate an unavailable verified source listing with an untestable target; inspect proxy history, bytecode, and build provenance. |
| 9 | **The delivered signing UI or wallet extension differed from its expected artifact** | **2, in a narrow pre-loss window**: [Bybit/Safe's targeted JavaScript](https://www.nccgroup.com/research/in-depth-technical-analysis-of-the-bybit-hack/) and [Trust Wallet v2.68](https://trustwallet.com/blog/company/trust-wallet-browser-extension-v268-incident-community-update) | Fetch the actual client artifact as different users, compare it to an approved build, and independently verify what signers authorize. This would detect malicious publication, not predict the earlier private compromise. |
| 10 | **A thin collateral market and insufficiently independent oracle sources** | **1**: [Nostra](https://blocksec.com/blog/web3-security-multicall-router-nostra-exploits) | Test whether one manipulable venue can determine borrowing power; count source independence, not merely the number of observations. |

**Operational conclusion:** the externally fruitful work in these cases was concentrated in deployed-code invariants, upgrades, oracle dependencies, and shipped client artifacts. The highest-dollar signer and endpoint incidents often had **no confirmed public warning before compromise**, so an external program should distinguish passive discovery from explicitly scoped tests of signing, support, contractor, and release controls rather than claiming a recon pipeline could have seen everything.
